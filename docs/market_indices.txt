Stock Market Indices Overview

Stock market indices track the performance of specific groups of stocks, providing benchmarks for overall market health and investment performance.

Major US Indices

S&P 500
The S&P 500 tracks 500 large-cap US companies across all sectors, making it the most widely followed broad market indicator. It's market-cap weighted, meaning larger companies like Apple and Microsoft have greater influence on the index's performance.

NASDAQ Composite
The NASDAQ Composite includes over 3,000 companies listed on the NASDAQ exchange. It's heavily weighted toward technology stocks, making it particularly sensitive to tech sector performance. Companies like Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, and NVIDIA drive much of its movement.

Dow Jones Industrial Average
The Dow comprises 30 blue-chip stocks selected to represent the American economy. Unlike most modern indices, it's price-weighted - stocks with higher share prices have more influence regardless of company size. It's the oldest US market index, dating back to 1896.

Russell 2000
The Russell 2000 measures small-cap company performance, tracking approximately 2,000 smaller publicly traded companies. It serves as a benchmark for the small-business segment of the US economy and often moves differently from large-cap indices.

Major Global Indices

FTSE 100
The FTSE 100 lists the 100 largest companies on the London Stock Exchange by market capitalization. It includes multinational corporations like Shell, AstraZeneca, and HSBC. About 70% of constituent revenues come from outside the UK.

DAX
Germany's DAX index tracks 30 major companies including Volkswagen, Siemens, and SAP. It reflects the performance of Europe's largest economy and is closely watched for signals about European market health.

Nikkei 225
The Nikkei covers 225 blue-chip companies on the Tokyo Stock Exchange, including Toyota, Sony, and SoftBank. Like the Dow, it's price-weighted. The index famously peaked in 1989 and took decades to recover.

Hang Seng
The Hang Seng tracks about 50 of the largest companies in Hong Kong, including major Chinese firms. It serves as a key indicator for Asian market sentiment and Chinese economic performance.

Index Weighting Methods
Market-cap weighted indices (S&P 500, NASDAQ) give larger companies more influence based on total market value. Price-weighted indices (Dow Jones, Nikkei) weight components by share price. Equal-weighted indices give all components the same influence regardless of size.

Global Trading Hours
US markets operate 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM EST with pre-market (4:00-9:30 AM) and after-hours (4:00-8:00 PM) sessions. London trades 8:00 AM to 4:30 PM GMT. Tokyo operates 9:00 AM to 3:00 PM JST. Hong Kong runs 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM HKT. Time zone differences allow for nearly 24-hour global market activity.
