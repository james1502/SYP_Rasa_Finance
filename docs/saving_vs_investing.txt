# Saving vs. Investing: What's the Difference?

Saving is setting aside money for short-term needs with minimal risk. Investing is putting money to work for long-term growth, accepting higher risk.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
- Saving: For goals under 3-5 years (e.g., vacation, emergency fund). Keep it in a high-yield savings account or money market fund.
- Investing: For goals 5+ years away (retirement, a child's education). Use stocks, bonds, or funds.
- Why not keep everything in cash? Inflation eats your savings over time.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
- **Emergency fund**: 3-6 months of living expenses in liquid, safe savings - before any investing.
- **Short-term vs. long-term portfolio**: Short-term money should avoid stock market volatility.
- **Risk-return trade-off**: Savings are stable but low return; investments can double or halve quickly.
- **CDs and government bonds** bridge the gap: better yield than savings but with some lockup period.
- **Psychology**: Seeing investment balances drop may cause panic selling; only invest money you won't need soon.

## ADVANCED LEVEL
- **Cash drag**: Holding too much cash in a long-term portfolio reduces overall returns.
- **Inflation-adjusted emergency fund**: Use T-bills or TIPS to partially protect purchasing power.
- **Bucketing strategy**: Segment wealth by time horizon - cash (0-2 years), bonds (2-5 years), stocks (5+ years).
- **Sequence-of-returns mitigation**: Near retirement, shifting 2-3 years' withdrawals to cash/bonds can protect against selling stocks in a downturn.
- **Opportunity cost**: During market crashes, a well-funded emergency reserve prevents selling assets at lows.
