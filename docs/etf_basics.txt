Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) Guide

What is an ETF?
An Exchange-Traded Fund (ETF) is an investment fund that trades on stock exchanges, similar to individual stocks. ETFs hold assets such as stocks, bonds, commodities, or a mix, and generally track an underlying index.

Key Features:
- Trades like a stock on exchanges
- Typically tracks an index or sector
- Offers diversification in single purchase
- Generally lower fees than mutual funds
- Transparent holdings
- Tax efficient

ETF vs Mutual Fund:

ETFs:
- Trade throughout the day
- Real-time pricing
- Generally lower expense ratios
- No minimum investment (1 share)
- More tax efficient
- Can be shorted/margined

Mutual Funds:
- Trade once daily at NAV
- End-of-day pricing
- Often higher fees
- May have minimums ($1,000+)
- Less tax efficient
- Limited trading flexibility

Types of ETFs:

Index ETFs:
- Track market indices (S&P 500, NASDAQ)
- Examples: SPY, VOO, QQQ, IVV
- Lowest cost, most liquid
- Core portfolio holdings

Sector ETFs:
- Focus on specific industries
- Technology: XLK, VGT
- Healthcare: XLV, VHT
- Financials: XLF, VFH
- Energy: XLE, VDE

Bond ETFs:
- Track fixed income markets
- Treasury: TLT, IEF, SHY
- Corporate: LQD, HYG
- Municipal: MUB
- International: BNDX

International ETFs:
- Developed Markets: EFA, VEA
- Emerging Markets: EEM, VWO
- Country-specific: EWJ (Japan), FXI (China)
- Regional: VGK (Europe)

Commodity ETFs:
- Gold: GLD, IAU
- Silver: SLV
- Oil: USO
- Broad commodities: DJP

Thematic ETFs:
- Clean energy: ICLN, QCLN
- Cybersecurity: HACK, CIBR
- Blockchain: BLOK
- AI/Robotics: BOTZ, ROBO

Factor ETFs (Smart Beta):
- Value: VTV, IWD
- Growth: VUG, IWF
- Momentum: MTUM
- Quality: QUAL
- Dividend: VIG, SCHD

Leveraged and Inverse ETFs:
- 2x/3x leveraged: TQQQ, SQQQ
- Inverse: SH, PSQ
- Daily rebalancing (not for long-term)
- Higher fees and risks

Cryptocurrency ETFs:
- Bitcoin Spot ETFs: IBIT, FBTC, GBTC
- Ethereum ETFs: ETHA, FETH
- Provide crypto exposure without direct ownership

Key ETF Metrics:

Expense Ratio:
- Annual management fee
- Index ETFs: 0.03-0.20%
- Active ETFs: 0.50-1.00%
- Lower is better

Assets Under Management (AUM):
- Total value of ETF assets
- Larger = more liquid, tighter spreads
- Prefer ETFs with $100M+ AUM

Trading Volume:
- Average daily shares traded
- Higher volume = better liquidity
- Lower bid-ask spreads

Tracking Error:
- Deviation from underlying index
- Lower is better
- Affected by fees, sampling, timing

Bid-Ask Spread:
- Difference between buy and sell price
- Lower = lower trading cost
- Larger ETFs have tighter spreads

ETF Investment Strategies:

Core-Satellite:
- Core: Low-cost broad market ETFs (70-80%)
- Satellite: Sector/thematic ETFs (20-30%)
- Combines passive and active approaches

Dollar-Cost Averaging:
- Invest fixed amount regularly
- Works well with commission-free ETFs
- Reduces timing risk

Sector Rotation:
- Shift between sectors based on economic cycle
- Defensive in recession, cyclical in expansion
- Requires market timing ability

Asset Allocation:
- Mix of stock, bond, and other ETFs
- Rebalance periodically
- Adjust based on age and risk tolerance

Popular ETF Portfolios:

Three-Fund Portfolio:
- US Stocks (VTI): 60%
- International Stocks (VXUS): 30%
- Bonds (BND): 10%

Lazy Portfolio:
- Simple, diversified, low-cost
- Rebalance annually
- Set and forget approach

Tax Considerations:
- ETFs generally tax-efficient
- In-kind creation/redemption minimizes gains
- Qualified dividends taxed favorably
- Consider tax-loss harvesting
- Hold in appropriate account types

Risks:
- Market risk (value can decline)
- Tracking error
- Liquidity risk (smaller ETFs)
- Closure risk (ETF may close)
- Leveraged ETF decay
- Currency risk (international ETFs)

Choosing ETFs:
1. Define investment goal
2. Compare expense ratios
3. Check AUM and volume
4. Review tracking error
5. Consider tax efficiency
6. Evaluate issuer reputation (Vanguard, iShares, SPDR)
