# Risk Management Fundamentals

Risk management is the process of identifying, assessing, and controlling threats to your investment capital.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
Risk is the possibility of losing money. The faster you drive (higher risk), the bigger the potential crash.  
- Market risk: overall market declines.  
- Company risk: a single business failing.  
- Inflation risk: purchasing power loss.  
- Liquidity risk: inability to sell quickly.

Basic rules: don’t invest money you need within 5 years, diversify, keep a cash emergency fund.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
Measuring risk:  
- **Standard deviation**: Higher = more volatile.  
- **Beta**: >1 means more volatile than the market.  
- **Sharpe ratio**: Return per unit of risk.  
- **Maximum drawdown**: Peak‑to‑trough decline.

Position sizing: Never risk more than 1‑2% of your portfolio on a single trade.  
Formula: Position size = (Portfolio × Risk%) / (Entry – Stop loss).

## ADVANCED LEVEL
- **Value at Risk (VaR)**: Maximum expected loss at a given confidence level.  
- **Conditional VaR** (CVaR) looks at average loss beyond VaR.  
- **Kelly Criterion** optimises bet size for repeated gambles.  
- Tail‑risk hedging using out‑of‑the‑money puts, gold, or trend following.
