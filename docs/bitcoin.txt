Bitcoin (BTC) - The First Cryptocurrency

Overview:
Bitcoin is the world's first decentralized digital currency, launched in January 2009 by an anonymous person or group using the pseudonym Satoshi Nakamoto. It operates on a blockchain, a distributed ledger technology that allows peer-to-peer transactions without the need for a central authority like a bank or government.

Technology:
Bitcoin uses blockchain technology, which is essentially a public ledger of all transactions ever made with Bitcoin. Each block in the chain contains multiple transactions, and once a block is added to the chain, it cannot be altered. This makes Bitcoin transactions transparent and secure.

Mining:
Bitcoin mining is the process by which new bitcoins are created and transactions are verified and added to the blockchain. Miners use powerful computers to solve complex mathematical problems. The first miner to solve the problem gets to add a new block to the blockchain and is rewarded with newly created bitcoins (currently 6.25 BTC per block as of 2024) plus transaction fees.

The mining process uses a proof-of-work consensus mechanism, which requires significant computational power. This makes the network secure because an attacker would need to control more than 50% of the network's computing power to compromise it.

Supply and Scarcity:
Bitcoin has a maximum supply of 21 million coins, making it a scarce digital asset. As of 2025, approximately 19.7 million bitcoins have been mined. The rate of new bitcoin creation decreases over time through events called "halvings," which occur approximately every four years. The next halving is expected in 2028.

This scarcity is one reason many investors view Bitcoin as "digital gold" - a store of value that could protect against inflation.

Security:
Bitcoin transactions are secured through cryptographic techniques. Each user has a private key (like a password) and a public key (like an account number). To send Bitcoin, you need your private key. Transactions are verified by network nodes through cryptography and recorded on the blockchain.

Use Cases:
1. Store of Value: Many investors hold Bitcoin as a long-term investment, similar to gold.
2. Payment Method: Bitcoin can be used to purchase goods and services from merchants that accept it.
3. Remittances: Bitcoin enables fast, low-cost international money transfers.
4. Hedge Against Inflation: Some view Bitcoin as protection against currency devaluation.

Advantages:
- Decentralization: No single entity controls Bitcoin
- Transparency: All transactions are publicly visible on the blockchain
- Security: Cryptographic protection makes it very secure
- Limited Supply: Only 21 million will ever exist
- Borderless: Can be sent anywhere in the world quickly

Challenges:
- Price Volatility: Bitcoin's price can fluctuate significantly
- Scalability: The network can process only about 7 transactions per second
- Energy Consumption: Mining requires substantial electricity
- Regulatory Uncertainty: Different countries have different approaches to regulation

Market Information (2025):
Recent price: Bitcoin reached $65,000 in June 2025, driven by institutional interest, the approval of Bitcoin ETFs, and growing adoption by major companies. The total market capitalization of Bitcoin exceeds $1.2 trillion.

Historical Context:
- 2009: Bitcoin launched, first block mined
- 2010: First real-world Bitcoin transaction (10,000 BTC for two pizzas)
- 2017: Bitcoin reached $20,000 for the first time
- 2021: Bitcoin hit all-time high near $69,000
- 2024: Multiple Bitcoin ETFs approved in the United States
- 2025: Continued institutional adoption and price growth

The Bitcoin Network:
The Bitcoin network consists of nodes (computers running Bitcoin software) spread across the globe. These nodes validate transactions and maintain copies of the blockchain. Anyone can run a node, contributing to the network's decentralization and security.

Future Outlook:
Bitcoin continues to evolve with developments like the Lightning Network, which aims to make transactions faster and cheaper. As adoption grows and technology improves, Bitcoin may play an increasingly important role in the global financial system.
