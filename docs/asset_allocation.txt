# Building an Asset Allocation Plan

Asset allocation is how you divide your investment portfolio among different asset classes, such as stocks, bonds, and cash.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
Think of a pizza divided into slices: one slice for stocks, one for bonds, one for cash. More stocks = more growth potential but bigger ups and downs. A simple starting point: subtract your age from 100 to get the % of stocks. A 30‑year‑old would have 70% stocks and 30% bonds.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
- Risk tolerance questionnaire: Helps determine the right mix based on when you need the money and how you react to losses.
- Core asset classes: Domestic stocks, international stocks, investment‑grade bonds, real estate, cash.
- Model portfolios:
  - Conservative: 30% stocks / 70% bonds
  - Moderate: 60% stocks / 40% bonds
  - Aggressive: 80%+ stocks
- Rebalancing: Once a year, sell winners and buy underweight assets to return to your target.
- Target‑date funds automatically shift allocation as the target year approaches.

## ADVANCED LEVEL
- Efficient frontier: Optimize asset mix for maximum return per unit of risk (Sharpe ratio).
- Factor‑based allocation: Tilting towards value, size, momentum, quality.
- Dynamic allocation: Adjusting based on market valuations (e.g., Shiller CAPE) or economic cycle.
- Glide path in retirement: Reducing equities gradually before and after retirement to manage sequence risk.
- Non‑traditional assets: REITs, commodities, private equity can improve diversification but may add illiquidity.
- Policy statement: Document your target allocation, rebalancing rules, and review process to avoid emotional decisions.
