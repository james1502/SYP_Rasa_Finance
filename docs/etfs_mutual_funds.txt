# ETFs vs Mutual Funds

Both pool money from many investors to buy a diversified basket of assets. They differ in trading, fees, and tax treatment.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
Think of a fruit basket – an ETF or mutual fund is like buying the whole basket instead of picking individual fruits (stocks). You get instant diversification.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
| Feature | ETF | Mutual Fund |
|---------|-----|-------------|
| Trading | Intraday on exchange | Once per day after close |
| Minimum | 1 share (often <$100) | Often $1,000+ |
| Expense ratio | Typically 0.03%–0.25% | Often 0.5%–1.5% |
| Tax efficiency | More tax‑efficient | Can distribute capital gains |

**Example**: SPY (SPDR S&P 500 ETF) tracks the S&P 500. Buying 1 share of SPY at $500 gives you exposure to 500 large US companies.

## ADVANCED LEVEL
ETF creation/redemption happens in‑kind with authorized participants, which minimises capital gains distributions.  
- **Bid‑ask spread** and **premium/discount to NAV** affect real cost.  
- **Liquidity** depends on underlying asset liquidity, not just ETF volume.  
- Advanced structures: physically replicated vs. synthetic (swap‑based) ETFs.  
- Active ETFs are rising but still represent a fraction of the market.
