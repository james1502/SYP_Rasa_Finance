# Fundamental Analysis: The Bigger Picture

Fundamental analysis evaluates a company's true worth by examining financial statements, competitive position, and economic environment.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
You're a detective trying to find out if a company is healthy. You look at how much money it makes, how much it owes, and whether it has a durable advantage (a "moat"). The goal: buy shares for less than the company is truly worth.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
- **Quantitative factors**: Revenue, earnings, cash flow, debt, profit margins, P/E ratio.
- **Qualitative factors**: Management quality, brand power, patents, industry tailwinds.
- **Economic moat**: A sustainable competitive advantage (e.g., Coca-Cola's brand, Google's search dominance).
- **Management assessment**: Honest, competent leaders who allocate capital wisely.
- **Competitive analysis**: Porter's Five Forces - barriers to entry, supplier power, buyer power, substitute threats, rivalry.

## ADVANCED LEVEL
- **Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)**: The gold standard for estimating intrinsic value, heavily sensitive to growth and discount rate assumptions.
- **Reverse DCF**: Derives the growth rate implied by the current stock price.
- **Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP)**: Values conglomerates by appraising each business segment separately.
- **Normalized earnings**: Adjusting cyclical earnings to estimate sustainable profit.
- **Behavioral edge**: Markets often misprice stocks due to overreaction; fundamental analysis attempts to exploit that gap.
