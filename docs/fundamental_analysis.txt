Fundamental Analysis Guide

What is Fundamental Analysis?
Fundamental analysis is a method of evaluating securities by examining underlying economic, financial, and qualitative factors. The goal is to determine a security's intrinsic value and compare it to the current market price to identify investment opportunities.

Core Philosophy:
- Every stock has an intrinsic value
- Market price can deviate from intrinsic value
- Over time, price tends to reflect intrinsic value
- Buy undervalued, sell overvalued

Two Approaches:

Top-Down Analysis:
1. Global economy assessment
2. Country/regional analysis
3. Industry/sector analysis
4. Individual company analysis

Bottom-Up Analysis:
1. Start with company financials
2. Compare to industry peers
3. Consider broader economic context

Key Financial Statements:

Income Statement (P&L):
- Revenue/Sales: Total income from operations
- Gross Profit: Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold
- Operating Income: Gross Profit - Operating Expenses
- Net Income: Bottom line profit after all expenses
- Earnings Per Share (EPS): Net Income / Shares Outstanding

Balance Sheet:
- Assets: What the company owns
- Liabilities: What the company owes
- Shareholders' Equity: Assets - Liabilities
- Book Value: Total equity / Shares outstanding

Cash Flow Statement:
- Operating Cash Flow: Cash from core business
- Investing Cash Flow: Capital expenditures, investments
- Financing Cash Flow: Debt, equity, dividends
- Free Cash Flow: Operating CF - Capital Expenditures

Key Valuation Ratios:

Price-to-Earnings (P/E):
- Stock Price / EPS
- Forward P/E uses projected earnings
- Compare to industry average and historical
- High P/E may indicate overvaluation or growth expectations

Price-to-Book (P/B):
- Stock Price / Book Value per Share
- Useful for asset-heavy companies
- P/B < 1 may indicate undervaluation
- Less relevant for tech/service companies

Price-to-Sales (P/S):
- Market Cap / Annual Revenue
- Useful for unprofitable companies
- Lower is generally better
- Compare within same industry

Price-to-Earnings-Growth (PEG):
- P/E Ratio / EPS Growth Rate
- PEG < 1 may indicate undervaluation
- Accounts for growth expectations
- Useful for growth stock comparison

Enterprise Value/EBITDA (EV/EBITDA):
- EV = Market Cap + Debt - Cash
- EBITDA = Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization
- Better for comparing companies with different capital structures
- Common in M&A valuation

Profitability Ratios:

Return on Equity (ROE):
- Net Income / Shareholders' Equity
- Measures profit generation from equity
- Higher is better (compare to industry)
- 15%+ considered good for many industries

Return on Assets (ROA):
- Net Income / Total Assets
- How efficiently assets generate profit
- Industry-specific benchmarks

Gross Margin:
- Gross Profit / Revenue
- Higher indicates pricing power
- Should be stable or improving

Net Profit Margin:
- Net Income / Revenue
- Overall profitability measure
- Varies significantly by industry

Financial Health Ratios:

Debt-to-Equity (D/E):
- Total Debt / Shareholders' Equity
- Lower generally means less risk
- Industry-specific standards
- Too low may indicate underleverage

Current Ratio:
- Current Assets / Current Liabilities
- Measures short-term liquidity
- > 1 indicates ability to pay short-term debts
- 1.5-2.0 often considered healthy

Interest Coverage:
- EBIT / Interest Expense
- Ability to pay interest on debt
- > 3 generally considered safe

Qualitative Factors:

Management Quality:
- Track record and experience
- Capital allocation decisions
- Insider ownership
- Compensation alignment

Competitive Position:
- Market share
- Barriers to entry
- Brand strength
- Patents and intellectual property

Industry Analysis:
- Porter's Five Forces
- Industry growth rate
- Regulatory environment
- Cyclicality

Economic Moat:
- Sustainable competitive advantages
- Types: brand, cost, network effects, switching costs
- Wider moat = more durable profits

Valuation Methods:

Discounted Cash Flow (DCF):
- Project future cash flows
- Discount to present value
- Sum gives intrinsic value
- Sensitive to assumptions

Comparable Company Analysis:
- Value relative to similar companies
- Use multiple ratios
- Adjust for differences

Dividend Discount Model:
- For dividend-paying stocks
- Value = Dividend / (Required Return - Growth Rate)
- Gordon Growth Model variation

Limitations:
1. Based on historical data and projections
2. Subjective assumptions required
3. Doesn't account for market sentiment
4. Time-consuming research
5. May miss short-term opportunities
6. Accounting manipulation possible

Best Practices:
1. Use multiple valuation methods
2. Understand the business model
3. Read annual reports (10-K, 10-Q)
4. Listen to earnings calls
5. Compare to competitors
6. Consider margin of safety
7. Update analysis regularly
