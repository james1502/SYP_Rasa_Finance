ASX 200 Index (Australia) Guide

What is the ASX 200?
The S&P/ASX 200 is Australia's primary stock market benchmark, tracking the 200 largest companies listed on the Australian Securities Exchange (ASX). It represents approximately 80% of Australia's total equity market capitalization.

Key Facts:
- Launched: March 31, 2000
- Operator: S&P Dow Jones Indices (with ASX)
- Base Value: 3,133.3 points (March 31, 2000)
- Constituents: 200 companies
- Calculation: Free-float market capitalization weighted
- Review: Quarterly rebalancing

Index Composition by Sector:
- Financials: ~30% (Major banks dominate)
- Materials: ~20% (Mining companies)
- Healthcare: ~10%
- Consumer Discretionary: ~8%
- Industrials: ~7%
- Real Estate: ~7%
- Energy: ~5%
- Technology: ~4%
- Others: ~9%

Major ASX 200 Constituents:
- BHP Group (Mining - largest by weight)
- Commonwealth Bank (Banking)
- CSL Limited (Biotechnology)
- National Australia Bank
- Westpac Banking
- ANZ Banking Group

Trading Hours:
- Pre-open: 7:00 AM - 10:00 AM (AEST)
- Normal Trading: 10:00 AM - 4:00 PM (AEST)
- Closing Auction: 4:00 PM - 4:12 PM (AEST)
- Note: Different time zones vs US/Europe

ASX Index Family:
- S&P/ASX 200: Main benchmark
- S&P/ASX 20: Top 20 companies
- S&P/ASX 50: Top 50 companies
- S&P/ASX 300: Broader coverage
- S&P/ASX Small Ordinaries: Small-caps
- S&P/ASX All Technology Index

Unique Characteristics:

Resource-Heavy:
- Major iron ore, gold, and LNG exposure
- BHP and Rio Tinto significant weights
- Commodity prices directly impact index
- Benefits from global infrastructure spending

Banking Dominance:
- "Big Four" banks: CBA, NAB, Westpac, ANZ
- Combined ~25% of index weight
- High dividend yields
- Sensitive to Australian property market

Factors Affecting ASX 200:
1. Commodity prices (iron ore, gold, copper)
2. Chinese economic demand (major export market)
3. RBA interest rate decisions
4. Australian dollar movements
5. Domestic housing market
6. Global risk sentiment
7. US market direction

Investment Options:
- SPDR S&P/ASX 200 Fund (STW)
- iShares Core S&P/ASX 200 ETF (IOZ)
- Vanguard Australian Shares ETF (VAS)
- ASX 200 Futures (SPI 200)
- US-listed Australia ETFs (EWA)

Dividend Culture:
- Australian companies known for high dividends
- Franking credits: Tax advantages for Australian investors
- Typical yield: 4-5%
- Banks and miners are major dividend payers

Economic Significance:
- Australia: 13th largest economy globally
- Major commodity exporter
- Strong ties to Asian economies
- Stable political/economic environment
- AAA credit rating

Advantages:
- Resource sector exposure
- High dividend yields
- Developed market stability
- Different time zone diversification
- China growth proxy

Risks:
- China economic dependency
- Commodity price volatility
- Concentrated sectors (banks, mining)
- Currency risk (AUD)
- Housing market exposure

Historical Performance:
- 2007: Pre-GFC peak around 6,800
- 2009: GFC low around 3,100
- 2020: COVID crash and recovery
- Record highs in 2024

Comparison with Other Indices:
- More commodity-focused than US indices
- Higher dividend yield than most developed markets
- Banking sector larger weight than peers
- Limited technology exposure vs S&P 500
