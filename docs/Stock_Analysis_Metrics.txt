# Understanding Stock Analysis Metrics

When you analyze a stock, you look at a mix of price data, performance trends, technical signals, valuation ratios, and trading volume. Together they help you decide if a stock is worth buying, holding, or selling.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
Think of a stock analysis like a health check-up.  
- **Price** is the current "pulse" - what you pay for one share right now.  
- **Market Cap** tells you the size of the company (small, mid, or large).  
- **Performance (YTD, 1-month)** shows how much the stock has gone up or down recently.  
- **Technical indicators** like moving averages and RSI give a sense of whether the stock is overbought (too expensive) or oversold (too cheap).  
- **Valuation** (P/E, Forward P/E, PEG) tells you if the stock is fairly priced relative to what the company earns.  
- **Volume** shows how many shares are being traded - like checking how crowded a shop is.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
Here is what each metric means:

**Price & Market Cap**  
- *Price*: The current trading price of one share.  
- *Market Cap*: Price  total shares outstanding. A quick measure of company size (large-cap >$10 billion; mid-cap $2-10 billion; small-cap <$2 billion).

**Performance**  
- *YTD (Year-to-Date)*: The percentage change in price from the first trading day of the year to today.  
- *1-Month Performance*: The percentage change over the last 30 calendar days.

**Technical Indicators**  
- *20-day SMA (Simple Moving Average)*: The average closing price over the last 20 trading days. Shows short-term trend.  
- *50-day SMA*: The average closing price over the last 50 trading days. Shows medium-term trend.  
  - Price above the SMA often suggests an uptrend; below suggests a downtrend.  
- *RSI (Relative Strength Index)*: A momentum oscillator from 0 to 100.  
  - Above 70 = overbought (might pull back).  
  - Below 30 = oversold (might bounce).

**Valuation**  
- *P/E (Price-to-Earnings)*: Stock price divided by earnings per share (EPS). How much you pay for $1 of profit.  
  - High P/E could mean high growth expectations or overvaluation.  
- *Forward P/E*: Uses forecasted future EPS instead of past earnings. Helps account for expected growth.  
- *PEG (Price/Earnings to Growth)*: P/E divided by the annual EPS growth rate.  
  - PEG < 1 is often considered undervalued relative to growth.

**Volume vs. Average Volume**  
- *Volume*: Total shares traded in the current session (or most recent session).  
- *Average Volume*: Usually a 10-day or 3-month daily average.  
  - Higher-than-average volume on a price move suggests stronger conviction.  
  - Low volume rallies may lack staying power.

## ADVANCED LEVEL
- **Price vs. Intrinsic Value**: Current price may differ from fair value estimated by Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) models. Use valuation metrics as a quick check, not a full thesis.  
- **Market Cap biases**: Mega-cap stocks can be more stable but grow slower; micro-caps carry higher growth potential but higher risk.  
- **Performance in context**: YTD and 1-month returns must be compared to a benchmark (e.g., S&P 500) and the sector. A 10% gain is weak if the market rose 20%.  
- **SMA crossovers**: When the 20-day SMA crosses above the 50-day SMA, it's called a "golden cross"(bullish). The opposite is a "death cross"(bearish). These are lagging signals.  
- **RSI nuances**: In a strong uptrend, RSI can remain overbought for extended periods without a pullback. Use together with volume and price action.  
- **P/E limitations**: Earnings can be manipulated (non-GAAP vs. GAAP). P/E is meaningless for negative-earnings companies. Cyclical companies need normalized earnings.  
- **Forward P/E risk**: Analyst estimates can be too optimistic. Always check earnings revision trends.  
- **PEG pitfalls**: Growth rates are forecasts and can decline quickly. A low PEG with unstable growth is a value trap.  
- **Volume analysis**: Volume spikes on earnings days, news events, or option expiration can distort the average. Volume precedes price - unusual volume often signals upcoming moves.  
- **Putting it together**: A healthy stock often shows price above both SMAs, RSI in the 40-70 range, reasonable P/E relative to peers, and volume confirming the trend. Discrepancies (e.g., price rising but volume falling) demand caution.
