Stablecoins Guide

What are Stablecoins?
Stablecoins are cryptocurrencies designed to maintain a stable value, typically pegged to a fiat currency like the US dollar. They combine the benefits of cryptocurrency (fast transfers, programmability) with price stability.

Why Stablecoins Matter:
- Reduce volatility in crypto portfolios
- Enable DeFi without price fluctuation risk
- Facilitate trading between cryptocurrencies
- Provide stable medium of exchange
- Enable faster, cheaper cross-border payments

Types of Stablecoins:

1. Fiat-Collateralized:
Backed 1:1 by fiat currency reserves

USDT (Tether):
- Largest stablecoin by market cap
- Available on most blockchains
- Controversial reserve transparency history
- Most liquid stablecoin

USDC (USD Coin):
- Issued by Circle
- Regular audits and attestations
- Strong regulatory compliance
- Popular in DeFi and institutions

BUSD (Binance USD):
- Issued by Paxos for Binance
- Regulated by NYDFS
- Being phased out due to regulatory pressure

TUSD (TrueUSD):
- Real-time attestation of reserves
- Multi-bank reserve storage

2. Crypto-Collateralized:
Backed by cryptocurrency assets

DAI:
- Issued by MakerDAO
- Over-collateralized by crypto (ETH, WBTC, etc.)
- Decentralized governance
- $1 peg maintained through smart contracts
- Most popular decentralized stablecoin

LUSD (Liquity USD):
- Backed only by ETH
- Fully decentralized
- No governance token
- Minimum collateral ratio: 110%

3. Algorithmic Stablecoins:
Maintain peg through algorithms and incentives

FRAX:
- Partially collateralized, partially algorithmic
- Collateral ratio adjusts based on demand
- Has survived where others failed

Note: Pure algorithmic stablecoins (like Terra UST) have historically failed catastrophically. Most have been abandoned or redesigned.

4. Commodity-Backed:
Backed by physical assets

PAXG (Pax Gold):
- Each token = 1 troy ounce of gold
- Redeemable for physical gold
- Stored in London vaults

XAUT (Tether Gold):
- Also backed by physical gold
- Issued by Tether

How to Use Stablecoins:

Trading:
- Park funds between trades
- Quote currency on exchanges
- Reduce portfolio volatility

DeFi:
- Earn yield through lending (3-10% APY)
- Provide liquidity in stablecoin pools
- Borrow against crypto collateral

Payments:
- Send money globally
- Pay for goods and services
- Receive payments as freelancer

Savings:
- Earn higher yields than traditional banks
- Maintain dollar exposure in crypto ecosystem

Risks:

De-pegging Risk:
- Stablecoins can lose their $1 peg
- Happened to UST (crashed to $0)
- Even USDC briefly lost peg during SVB crisis

Regulatory Risk:
- Increasing government scrutiny
- Potential restrictions or bans
- Reserve requirements may change

Counterparty Risk:
- Trust in issuer to maintain reserves
- Bank failures can affect backing
- Smart contract risks for decentralized stablecoins

Centralization Risk:
- Fiat-backed stablecoins can freeze accounts
- Issuers can blacklist addresses
- Decentralized alternatives exist but have tradeoffs

Choosing a Stablecoin:
Consider:
- Transparency of reserves
- Regulatory compliance
- Blockchain availability
- DeFi integration
- Liquidity and trading volume
- Centralization vs decentralization preference

Best Practices:
1. Diversify across multiple stablecoins
2. Understand the backing mechanism
3. Monitor de-peg risks
4. Use reputable platforms
5. Consider decentralized options for censorship resistance
6. Keep updated on regulatory developments
