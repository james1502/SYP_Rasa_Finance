# Why Diversification Matters

Diversification means spreading your investments across different assets to reduce risk.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
Do not put all your eggs in one basket. If you invest in one company and it goes bankrupt, you lose everything. Across 100 companies, one failure is barely noticeable.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
True diversification goes beyond multiple stocks. You need assets with low correlation:
- Stocks, bonds, real estate, commodities, cash
- Geographic diversification: US, international, emerging markets
- Sector diversification: technology, healthcare, financials, energy

Holding 15 to 20 stocks across different sectors can eliminate most company-specific risk. ETFs provide instant diversification across hundreds of holdings.

Rebalancing annually restores your target allocation and maintains your desired risk level.

## ADVANCED LEVEL
Modern Portfolio Theory seeks to maximize return for a given risk level by combining uncorrelated assets.
- Efficient frontier: The set of optimal portfolios offering the best risk-return tradeoff
- Sharpe ratio: Measures excess return per unit of risk
- Maximum drawdown: Largest peak-to-trough decline
- Tail risk: Probability of extreme losses beyond normal volatility

Alternative assets like private equity and hedge funds can improve the efficient frontier but often come with liquidity constraints and higher fees.

Correlation analysis helps identify truly diversified holdings. Assets with correlation below 0.5 provide meaningful diversification benefits.
