# What Is a Stock?

A stock (or equity) represents fractional ownership in a company. When you buy a stock, you own a piece of that business and may profit if the company grows.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
Think of a company as a pizza – each share is one slice. If the pizza gets bigger (the company becomes more valuable), your slice is worth more.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
Stocks trade on exchanges like the NYSE or NASDAQ.  
- **Common stock**: Voting rights + potential dividends.  
- **Preferred stock**: No voting rights, but fixed dividends and higher claim on assets in bankruptcy.

Key terms:  
- **Ticker**: The symbol that identifies a stock (e.g., AAPL = Apple).  
- **Market Cap**: Stock price × total shares outstanding.  
- **IPO**: Initial Public Offering – the first sale of shares to the public.

## ADVANCED LEVEL
Market cap categories:  
- Mega-cap: >$200 billion  
- Large-cap: $10–200 billion  
- Mid-cap: $2–10 billion  
- Small-cap: $300 million–$2 billion  
- Micro-cap: <$300 million

A company’s stock price is driven by earnings, growth, interest rates, and market sentiment. Discounted cash flow (DCF) analysis estimates intrinsic value. Behavioral biases often cause price to deviate from that value.
