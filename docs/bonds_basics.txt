# Bond Fundamentals

A bond is a loan you make to a government or corporation in exchange for regular interest payments and the return of principal at maturity.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
You lend $1,000 to your city for 10 years. The city pays you $40 every year (4% coupon), and after 10 years you get back your $1,000.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
Key terms:  
- **Face value (Par)**: What you are repaid at maturity (usually $1,000).  
- **Coupon rate**: The annual interest percentage.  
- **Yield to Maturity (YTM)**: The total return if held until maturity, which changes as the bond’s market price fluctuates.  
- **Credit rating**: AAA (safest) to D (in default).  
- **Duration**: Measures sensitivity to interest rate changes. Higher duration = bigger price swings.

## ADVANCED LEVEL
Yield curve shapes:  
- Normal (upward‑sloping) – long‑term yields higher than short‑term.  
- Inverted (short‑term yields higher) – often a recession signal.  
- Convexity improves on duration estimates for large yield changes.  
- Callable bonds: issuer can redeem early, introducing reinvestment risk.  
- Credit spreads widen (risk‑off) or narrow (risk‑on) reflecting market outlook.
