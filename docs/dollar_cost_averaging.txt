# Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)

Dollar-cost averaging means investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, regardless of price.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
Instead of investing 12000 dollars at once, you invest 1000 dollars each month for a year. When prices are low, your money buys more shares. When prices are high, it buys fewer shares. This smooths your purchase price over time.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
Mathematically, DCA lowers the average cost per share over time compared to the average price, but it does not guarantee a profit. It reduces the risk of timing disaster - investing a lump sum right before a market crash.
Studies show lump-sum investing usually outperforms DCA in rising markets, but DCA provides psychological comfort and discipline for investors.

## ADVANCED LEVEL
True DCA versus periodic investing: if you already have a lump sum, holding cash to DCA lowers expected returns because cash drag offsets the benefit.
DCA is most beneficial when volatility is high and markets are mean-reverting.
Combining DCA with value averaging (investing more when markets fall, less when they rise) can enhance returns but requires active monitoring.
In retirement decumulation, a reverse DCA strategy with systematic withdrawals helps manage sequence of returns risk.
