# Introduction to Technical Analysis

Technical analysis studies past price and volume data to forecast future price movements.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
If the stock market were a weather forecast, technical analysis would look at past weather patterns to guess tomorrow's weather. Charts show price history. "Support" is a price floor where buyers tend to step in; "resistance" is a ceiling where sellers appear.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
- **Trend**: A series of higher highs (uptrend) or lower lows (downtrend). "The trend is your friend."
- **Moving averages (MA)**: Smooth out price data. A rising 20-day MA signals short-term strength. When a short MA crosses above a longer MA, it's a "golden cross" (bullish).
- **Relative Strength Index (RSI)**: Measures momentum on a scale of 0-100. Above 70 = overbought, below 30 = oversold.
- **Volume**: Heavy volume confirms a move, light volume questions it.
- **Common patterns**: Head and shoulders (reversal), cup and handle (continuation).

## ADVANCED LEVEL
- **Breadth indicators**: Advance-decline line, new highs/new lows. They reveal participation behind index moves.
- **Divergence**: Price makes a new high while RSI makes a lower high to momentum weakening.
- **Fibonacci retracements**: 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% levels that often act as support/resistance.
- **Intermarket analysis**: Bonds, currencies, and commodities all influence equity charts.
- **Limitations**: Chart patterns can be self-fulfilling but often fail; always combine with risk management.
