BSE Sensex Index (India) Guide

What is the Sensex?
The BSE Sensex (Sensitive Index) is India's oldest and most widely followed stock market index. It tracks 30 of the largest and most actively traded companies on the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE), representing key sectors of the Indian economy.

Key Facts:
- Founded: January 1, 1986
- Operator: BSE Limited (Bombay Stock Exchange)
- Base Year: 1978-79 (base value: 100)
- Constituents: 30 companies
- Calculation: Free-float market capitalization weighted
- Review: Semi-annual rebalancing

Index Composition by Sector:
- Financial Services: ~40%
- Information Technology: ~15%
- Consumer Goods: ~12%
- Oil & Gas: ~10%
- Automobiles: ~8%
- Healthcare: ~5%
- Others: ~10%

Major Sensex Constituents:
- Reliance Industries (Largest - diversified conglomerate)
- HDFC Bank (Private banking)
- ICICI Bank (Private banking)
- Infosys (IT services)
- TCS (Tata Consultancy Services - IT)
- Hindustan Unilever (Consumer goods)
- ITC Limited (Diversified)
- State Bank of India (Public bank)
- Bharti Airtel (Telecommunications)
- Kotak Mahindra Bank (Private banking)
- Bajaj Finance (NBFC)
- Larsen & Toubro (Engineering)

Trading Hours:
- Pre-open: 9:00 AM - 9:15 AM (IST)
- Regular Trading: 9:15 AM - 3:30 PM (IST)
- Post-close: 3:40 PM - 4:00 PM (IST)

Indian Index Family:
- BSE Sensex: 30 large-caps on BSE
- Nifty 50: 50 large-caps on NSE (National Stock Exchange)
- BSE 100, BSE 200, BSE 500: Broader indices
- BSE Midcap, BSE Smallcap
- Sectoral indices (Bankex, IT, Healthcare)

Sensex vs Nifty 50:
- Both track large Indian companies
- Nifty 50 has 50 stocks vs Sensex 30
- Nifty is more widely used for derivatives
- Strong correlation between both
- Sensex is older, Nifty more traded

Factors Affecting Sensex:
1. India GDP growth and economic data
2. RBI (Reserve Bank of India) monetary policy
3. Foreign Institutional Investor (FII) flows
4. Crude oil prices (India imports most oil)
5. Indian Rupee exchange rate
6. Government policy and reforms
7. Monsoon season (agriculture impact)
8. Global risk sentiment

Investment Options:
- Indian ETFs: SBI ETF Sensex, HDFC Sensex ETF
- US-listed India ETFs: INDA, SMIN, EPI
- Sensex Futures and Options on BSE
- Nifty derivatives more liquid

Economic Significance:
- India: 5th largest economy (by GDP)
- Fastest-growing major economy
- Large, young population (1.4+ billion)
- Growing middle class
- Digital transformation underway

India Growth Story:
- Manufacturing hub ("Make in India")
- IT and services powerhouse
- Infrastructure development
- Financial inclusion expansion
- Consumption-driven growth

Advantages:
- High growth potential
- Young demographics
- Large domestic market
- Reform momentum
- Diversification from developed markets

Risks:
- Currency volatility (INR)
- Oil price sensitivity
- Inflation concerns
- Bureaucratic challenges
- Geopolitical factors
- FII flow dependency

Historical Performance:
- 1986: Started at 549 points
- 1991: Liberalization reforms
- 2008: Global financial crisis impact
- 2020: COVID crash to ~26,000
- 2024: New highs above 70,000

Foreign Investment in India:
- FPIs (Foreign Portfolio Investors) important
- SEBI regulates foreign investment
- Tax implications for foreign investors
- ADRs available for some Indian companies

Comparison with Other Emerging Markets:
- Higher growth than most EM indices
- Less commodity-dependent than Brazil/Russia
- Different sector mix than China
- Strong corporate governance standards
- English-speaking business environment
