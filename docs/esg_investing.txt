ESG and Sustainable Investing

ESG investing considers Environmental, Social, and Governance factors alongside financial returns.

BEGINNER LEVEL

Environmental: How a company handles climate change, pollution, and resource use.

Social: Employee treatment, human rights, community impact.

Governance: Executive pay, board diversity, shareholder rights.

ESG investing is like choosing a restaurant not just for taste but also for how it treats its staff and sources ingredients.

INTERMEDIATE LEVEL

Screening: Negative screening excludes "sin stocks" like tobacco and weapons; positive screening seeks best-in-class ESG performers.

ESG ratings from agencies like MSCI and Sustainalytics are inconsistent - different agencies grade the same company differently.

Green bonds: Debt issued to fund specific environmental projects.

Performance: Historically, ESG funds have shown competitive returns with potentially lower downside risk.

Shareholder engagement: Using your ownership stake to influence company behaviour through proxy voting and resolutions.

ADVANCED LEVEL

Materiality: Not all ESG issues matter financially for every industry. Water usage matters more for beverage companies than for software firms.

Impact measurement: Quantifying real-world outcomes like tonnes of CO2 avoided remains challenging.

Greenwashing: Companies or funds overstate their ESG credentials. Scrutinise holdings and methodology.

Fiduciary duty and ESG: Debate over whether pension trustees can legally consider ESG if it "sacrifices" returns, though regulation is evolving.

Integration into valuation: Adjust discount rates or cash flows for ESG risks like carbon taxes and regulation.
