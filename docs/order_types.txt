# Understanding Order Types

When you buy or sell a stock, you give instructions called an "order." The type of order determines the price and timing of execution.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
- **Market order**: Buys or sells immediately at the current price. Fast, but you may get a slightly different price in a fast-moving market.
- **Limit order**: Sets a maximum price you'll pay (or minimum you'll sell). Guarantees price, but not execution.
- Example: A stock trades at $100. You place a limit buy at $98. The order only executes if the price drops to $98 or lower.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
- **Stop order (stop-loss)**: Triggers a market order once a certain price is hit. Often used to limit losses.
- **Stop-limit order**: Converts to a limit order when the stop price is reached, combining downside protection with price control.
- **Good-'til-cancelled (GTC) vs. day order**: GTC keeps the order alive until filled or cancelled (usually up to 6090 days).
- **All-or-none (AON)** and **fill-or-kill (FOK)**: AON requires the entire order to fill; FOK requires immediate complete fill or cancellation.

## ADVANCED LEVEL
- **Hidden/iceberg orders**: Institutional tools to mask large order size.
- **Slippage**: Difference between expected execution price and actual price, common in volatile or illiquid stocks.
- **Order routing**: How brokerages direct orders for best execution (smart order routing) vs. payment-for-order-flow (PFOF).
- **Algorithmic execution**: VWAP, TWAP, and implementation shortfall strategies for minimizing market impact.
