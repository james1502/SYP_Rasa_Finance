# How Taxes Affect Your Investments

Taxes take a slice of your investment returns. Knowing the rules helps you keep more of your money.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
- If you sell a stock for more than you paid, that profit is a "capital gain" and may be taxed.
- Owning in a retirement account (IRA, 401(k)) shields you from these taxes until withdrawal (or forever in a Roth).
- Dividends often count as income too, even if you reinvest them.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
- **Short‑term capital gains**: Assets held ≤1 year, taxed as ordinary income (up to 37%).
- **Long‑term capital gains**: Assets held >1 year, taxed at 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on your income.
- **Qualified dividends**: Taxed at the lower long‑term capital gains rate.
- **Wash sale rule**: You can’t claim a loss on a stock if you buy it back within 30 days before or after the sale.
- **Tax‑efficient fund placement**: Keep high‑tax assets (bonds, REITs) in tax‑advantaged accounts; low‑turnover equity ETFs in taxable accounts.

## ADVANCED LEVEL
- **Tax‑loss harvesting (TLH)**: Selling losing investments to offset gains, then reinvesting in similar (but not identical) assets.
- **Tax‑gain harvesting**: Realizing gains in a low‑income year when your long‑term capital gains rate is 0%.
- **Net investment income tax (NIIT)**: An additional 3.8% surtax on investment income for high earners.
- **ETF vs. mutual fund tax efficiency**: ETFs rarely distribute capital gains due to in‑kind redemption.
- **Donor‑advised funds (DAFs)** and **qualified charitable distributions** from IRAs for tax‑efficient giving.
