# Key Stock Valuation Metrics

Valuation metrics help determine if a stock is overvalued, undervalued, or fairly priced.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
- **P/E ratio** (Price‑to‑Earnings): Stock price ÷ earnings per share. A P/E of 15 means you pay $15 for $1 of earnings.  
- **P/B ratio** (Price‑to‑Book): Stock price ÷ book value per share. Often used for banks.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
- **Forward P/E**: Uses estimated future earnings, not past.  
- **PEG ratio** = P/E ÷ annual earnings growth rate. A PEG < 1 may indicate undervaluation.  
- **Dividend yield**: Annual dividend ÷ stock price.  
- **EV/EBITDA**: Enterprise value (market cap + debt – cash) divided by EBITDA. Useful for comparing companies with different capital structures.  
- **Price‑to‑Sales (P/S)**: For companies with no earnings.

## ADVANCED LEVEL
- Discounted Cash Flow (DCF): projects future free cash flows and discounts them back to present value.  
- Terminal value assumptions often dominate the DCF result.  
- Sensitivity analysis: small changes in growth or discount rate drastically change fair value.  
- Relative valuation (comps) vs. intrinsic valuation – using both provides a range.  
- Cyclical companies require normalized earnings over the cycle.
