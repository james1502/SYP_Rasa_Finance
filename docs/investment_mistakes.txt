# Common Investment Mistakes to Avoid

Learning from others’ mistakes is cheaper than making your own.

## BEGINNER LEVEL
1. Not starting early – compound growth needs time.  
2. Panic selling in downturns – locks in losses.  
3. Chasing hot stocks – often leads to buying at peaks.  
4. No emergency fund – forces you to sell investments at the wrong time.  
5. Ignoring fees – high fees compound against you.  
6. No diversification – one stock can wipe you out.  
7. Trying to time the market – missing the best days dramatically reduces returns.  
8. Following tips without research.  
9. Emotional investing – fear and greed cause poor decisions.  
10. No investment plan – random buying leads to random results.

## INTERMEDIATE LEVEL
Behavioral biases:  
- **Confirmation bias**: seeking evidence that supports your view.  
- **Recency bias**: overweighting recent events.  
- **Loss aversion**: pain from losses > pleasure from gains.  
- **Herd mentality**: following the crowd into bubbles.  
- **Overconfidence**: thinking you can beat the market consistently.  
- **Anchoring**: fixating on a past price rather than current value.

## ADVANCED LEVEL
- Overtrading: high turnover generates fees and taxes, hurting net returns.  
- Overleveraging: using margin or options to amplify bets can lead to total loss.  
- Survivorship bias: you only see the winners, not the many that failed.  
- Backtesting overfit: a strategy that works historically may fail out‑of‑sample.  
- Mitigation: maintain a written investment policy statement (IPS), automate contributions, and review performance against a benchmark annually.
