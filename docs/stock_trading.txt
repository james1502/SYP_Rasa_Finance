Stock Trading Fundamentals

Stock trading involves buying and selling shares of publicly traded companies. Understanding order types, trading hours, strategies, and risk management is essential for success.

Order Types

Market Orders
Market orders execute immediately at the current market price. A market buy order purchases shares at the best available ask price. A market sell order sells at the best available bid price. Market orders guarantee execution but not price, which can be problematic during volatile conditions.

Limit Orders
Limit orders specify the maximum price you'll pay when buying or minimum price you'll accept when selling. A buy limit order only executes at or below your specified price. A sell limit order only executes at or above your target. These orders control price but don't guarantee execution.

Stop-Loss Orders
Stop-loss orders automatically sell shares if the price falls to a specified level, limiting potential losses. Once triggered, they become market orders. For example, if you buy a stock at $100, you might set a stop-loss at $90 to cap your loss at 10%.

Stop-Limit Orders
Stop-limit orders combine stop and limit features. When the stop price is reached, the order becomes a limit order rather than a market order. This gives more price control but risks non-execution if prices gap through your limit.

Trading Sessions

Regular Session
The main trading session runs 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM Eastern Time on business days. This is when volume is highest and spreads are typically tightest.

Pre-Market Trading
Pre-market trading occurs 4:00 AM to 9:30 AM EST. Volume is lower and spreads wider. Price movements during this session can signal how the market will open.

After-Hours Trading
After-hours trading runs 4:00 PM to 8:00 PM EST. Like pre-market, liquidity is reduced and volatility can be higher. Earnings reports often release after hours, causing significant price moves.

Trading Strategies

Day Trading
Day trading involves buying and selling within the same trading day, closing all positions before market close. It requires significant time, attention, and carries high risk. Pattern day traders (4+ day trades in 5 days) must maintain $25,000 minimum account balance.

Swing Trading
Swing trading holds positions for several days to weeks, attempting to profit from short to medium-term price movements. It requires less time than day trading but still demands active monitoring and technical analysis skills.

Position Trading
Position trading takes a longer view, holding investments for months to years based on fundamental analysis. It's less time-intensive and typically carries lower risk than shorter-term strategies.

Dividend Investing
Dividend investing focuses on stocks that pay regular dividends, providing income alongside potential appreciation. Dividend aristocrats - companies that have increased dividends for 25+ consecutive years - are popular targets.

Risk Management

Portfolio Allocation
Avoid concentrating more than 5% of your portfolio in any single stock. This prevents one bad investment from devastating your entire portfolio.

Stop-Loss Discipline
Use stop-loss orders to automatically limit losses. A common approach is setting stops 7-10% below purchase price for growth stocks, or 15-20% for more volatile positions.

Diversification
Spread investments across different sectors (technology, healthcare, finance), market caps (large, mid, small), and asset classes (stocks, bonds, cash). This reduces exposure to any single risk factor.

Regular Rebalancing
Periodically rebalance your portfolio back to target allocations. Winners will grow to oversized positions while losers shrink, gradually shifting your risk profile if left unchecked.
