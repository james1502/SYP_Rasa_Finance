NASDAQ Composite Index

The NASDAQ Composite is a stock market index that includes almost all stocks listed on the NASDAQ stock exchange. It's heavily weighted toward technology and growth companies, making it a key indicator of the tech sector's health.

Key Facts:
- Launched in 1971
- Includes over 3,000 stocks listed on NASDAQ
- Market-cap weighted index
- Known for high concentration of technology stocks

Composition:
About 50% of the index is composed of technology companies, including software, semiconductors, internet services, and telecommunications. Other significant sectors include consumer services, healthcare, and financials.

Major Companies:
Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, NVIDIA, Alphabet, Meta, Tesla, Broadcom, and Netflix are among the largest constituents. The "Magnificent Seven" tech stocks have significant influence on index performance.

NASDAQ-100:
A subset tracking the 100 largest non-financial companies on NASDAQ. The QQQ ETF tracks this index and is popular among investors seeking tech exposure.

Volatility:
Due to its tech-heavy nature, the NASDAQ tends to be more volatile than broader indices like the S&P 500, with higher potential returns but greater risk.

Investment Options:
Investors can access the NASDAQ Composite through ETFs like QQQ (NASDAQ-100), ONEQ (Fidelity NASDAQ Composite), and NDAQ.
