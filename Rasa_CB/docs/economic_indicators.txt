Economic Indicators Guide

What are Economic Indicators?
Economic indicators are statistics that provide insights into the health and direction of an economy. They help investors, policymakers, and businesses make informed decisions about financial markets and economic trends.

Types of Indicators:

Leading Indicators:
- Change BEFORE the economy changes direction
- Help predict future economic activity
- Examples: Stock market, building permits, consumer confidence

Coincident Indicators:
- Change AT THE SAME TIME as the economy
- Confirm current economic state
- Examples: GDP, employment, industrial production

Lagging Indicators:
- Change AFTER the economy has changed
- Confirm trends that have occurred
- Examples: Unemployment rate, CPI inflation, corporate profits

Key Economic Indicators:

Gross Domestic Product (GDP):
- Total value of goods and services produced
- Most comprehensive economic measure
- Released quarterly
- Components: Consumption, Investment, Government, Net Exports
- Positive growth = Expanding economy
- Negative growth (2 quarters) = Technical recession

Employment Data:

Non-Farm Payrolls (US):
- Jobs added/lost in non-agricultural sectors
- Released first Friday of each month
- Major market-moving event
- 150-200K healthy, >250K strong

Unemployment Rate:
- Percentage of labor force unemployed
- Natural rate: ~4-5%
- Low unemployment: Tight labor market
- High unemployment: Economic weakness

Jobless Claims:
- Weekly initial unemployment claims
- Leading indicator of labor market
- Rising claims: Weakening economy
- <300K generally healthy

Inflation Indicators:

Consumer Price Index (CPI):
- Measures consumer price changes
- Released monthly
- Core CPI excludes food and energy
- Fed targets 2% inflation
- High inflation: Fed may raise rates
- Low inflation: Fed may cut rates

Producer Price Index (PPI):
- Wholesale price changes
- Leading indicator of CPI
- Shows supply-side pressures

Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE):
- Fed's preferred inflation measure
- Broader than CPI
- Released monthly

Consumer Indicators:

Consumer Confidence Index (CCI):
- Conference Board survey
- Measures consumer optimism
- Leading indicator of spending
- High confidence: Strong spending ahead

Consumer Sentiment (UMich):
- University of Michigan survey
- Similar to CCI
- Released twice monthly

Retail Sales:
- Monthly consumer spending data
- Critical for US (70% of GDP is consumption)
- Ex-autos and gas shows core trends

Business Indicators:

Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI):
- Survey of business conditions
- Manufacturing PMI and Services PMI
- Above 50: Expansion
- Below 50: Contraction
- ISM (US), S&P Global (international)

Industrial Production:
- Factory, mining, utility output
- Measures manufacturing health
- Capacity utilization important

Durable Goods Orders:
- Big-ticket item orders
- Leading indicator of business investment
- Volatile month-to-month
- Core (ex-transportation) more reliable

Housing Indicators:

Housing Starts:
- New residential construction begun
- Leading indicator
- Sensitive to interest rates

Building Permits:
- Permits issued for new construction
- Leading indicator of housing starts

Existing Home Sales:
- Sales of previously owned homes
- Largest segment of housing market

Home Price Indices:
- Case-Shiller, FHFA indices
- Track residential price changes
- Lagging indicator

Interest Rate Indicators:

Federal Funds Rate:
- Fed's target short-term rate
- Primary monetary policy tool
- Affects all borrowing costs
- FOMC meetings: 8 per year

Treasury Yields:
- Government borrowing costs
- 10-year yield: Benchmark for mortgages
- Yield curve shape important

Yield Curve:
- Normal: Longer rates > shorter rates
- Inverted: Shorter rates > longer rates
- Inversion often precedes recession

International Indicators:

Trade Balance:
- Exports minus imports
- Deficit: Imports > Exports
- Affects currency and GDP

Current Account:
- Broader than trade balance
- Includes services, income, transfers

Economic Calendar:

Major Releases (US):
- Employment Report: First Friday
- CPI: Mid-month
- GDP: End of month (quarterly)
- FOMC: 8 times/year
- PMI: Beginning of month
- Retail Sales: Mid-month

Using Economic Data:

For Stocks:
- Strong economy: Generally bullish
- Too strong: Inflation concerns
- Weak data: Recession fears
- Context matters more than single data points

For Bonds:
- Strong data: Yields rise (prices fall)
- Weak data: Yields fall (prices rise)
- Inflation data especially important

For Forex:
- Strong data: Currency strengthens
- Weak data: Currency weakens
- Relative to expectations crucial

For Crypto:
- Risk-on/risk-off sentiment
- Inflation hedge narrative
- Liquidity conditions matter

Trading Economic Data:

Consensus vs Actual:
- Markets price in expectations
- Surprise vs consensus moves markets
- Even good data can be "bad" if expected better

Pre-Release Positioning:
- Markets often move ahead of data
- "Buy the rumor, sell the news"
- Risk of wrong-sided positions

Post-Release Trading:
- Initial reaction may reverse
- Wait for dust to settle
- Look for confirmation
