Risk Management in Trading and Investing

What is Risk Management?
Risk management is the process of identifying, analyzing, and controlling potential losses in trading and investing. Proper risk management is crucial for long-term success and capital preservation.

Core Principles:
1. Protect capital first, profits second
2. Never risk more than you can afford to lose
3. Plan your trade, trade your plan
4. Cut losses quickly, let winners run

Position Sizing:

Percentage Risk Model:
- Risk only 1-2% of capital per trade
- Example: $100,000 account = max $1,000-2,000 risk per trade
- Allows for losing streaks without account destruction
- Professional traders often risk 0.5-1%

Fixed Dollar Amount:
- Risk same dollar amount per trade
- Simple but doesn't scale with account size
- Good for beginners with small accounts

Kelly Criterion:
- Mathematical formula for optimal bet size
- Kelly % = Win Rate - (Loss Rate / Win-Loss Ratio)
- Often use "half Kelly" for safety
- Aggressive approach, use cautiously

Volatility-Based Sizing:
- Adjust position size based on asset volatility
- Higher volatility = smaller position
- ATR (Average True Range) commonly used
- Normalizes risk across different assets

Stop-Loss Strategies:

Fixed Percentage Stop:
- Exit if price drops X% from entry
- Common: 5-10% for stocks, 2-5% for crypto
- Simple and systematic

Support/Resistance Stop:
- Place stop below support (longs) or above resistance (shorts)
- Technical basis for stop placement
- May be wider but more logical

ATR-Based Stop:
- Stop at 2-3x ATR from entry
- Accounts for normal volatility
- Less likely to be stopped out by noise

Trailing Stop:
- Follows price as it moves favorably
- Locks in profits while allowing upside
- Can be fixed % or ATR-based

Mental Stop:
- Not recommended - psychology makes execution difficult
- Always use hard stops

Take-Profit Strategies:

Fixed Risk-Reward Ratio:
- Target 2:1, 3:1 reward to risk
- If risking $100, target $200-300 profit
- Ensures profitable expectancy even with 50% win rate

Technical Targets:
- Next resistance/support level
- Fibonacci extensions
- Previous highs/lows

Scaling Out:
- Sell portions at different levels
- Example: 1/3 at 1R, 1/3 at 2R, 1/3 trailing
- Balances profit-taking with letting winners run

Diversification:

Asset Class Diversification:
- Stocks, bonds, commodities, crypto
- Different classes perform differently in various conditions
- Reduces overall portfolio volatility

Sector Diversification:
- Spread across industries
- Avoids concentration in one area
- Consider correlations between sectors

Geographic Diversification:
- Domestic and international exposure
- Reduces country-specific risk
- Currency exposure consideration

Time Diversification:
- Dollar-cost averaging
- Don't invest all at once
- Reduces timing risk

Correlation Considerations:
- Assets can become correlated in crises
- True diversification requires uncorrelated assets
- Regularly review correlation matrices

Risk Metrics:

Maximum Drawdown:
- Largest peak-to-trough decline
- Important for evaluating strategies
- Aim for drawdowns you can psychologically handle

Sharpe Ratio:
- Risk-adjusted return measure
- (Return - Risk Free Rate) / Standard Deviation
- Higher is better; > 1 is good, > 2 is very good

Sortino Ratio:
- Like Sharpe but only considers downside volatility
- Better for asymmetric return profiles

Value at Risk (VaR):
- Maximum expected loss at confidence level
- Example: 95% VaR of $10,000 means 5% chance of losing more
- Used by institutions

Psychological Risk Management:

Trading Plan:
- Define entry/exit rules before trading
- Stick to the plan during market hours
- Review and adjust only after hours

Trading Journal:
- Record every trade with reasoning
- Review mistakes and successes
- Identify patterns in behavior

Emotional Control:
- Don't trade when emotional
- Take breaks after big wins or losses
- Avoid revenge trading

Position Limits:
- Maximum positions at one time
- Maximum exposure to single asset
- Daily/weekly loss limits

Common Risk Management Mistakes:
1. No stop-loss ("hoping" it comes back)
2. Moving stops to avoid being stopped out
3. Overleveraging
4. Averaging down on losers
5. Risking too much per trade
6. Ignoring correlation risk
7. Not having a trading plan

Professional Practices:
1. Always know your maximum loss before entering
2. Size positions based on stop distance
3. Use stop-losses religiously
4. Review risk exposure daily
5. Stress test portfolio for various scenarios
6. Have circuit breakers for losing streaks
7. Maintain cash reserves for opportunities
