FTSE 100 Index

The FTSE 100 (Financial Times Stock Exchange 100) is a share index of the 100 largest companies listed on the London Stock Exchange by market capitalization. It's the most widely used indicator of the health of the UK stock market.

Key Facts:
- Launched on January 3, 1984, with a base level of 1,000
- Represents about 80% of the market cap of the entire London Stock Exchange
- Market-cap weighted index
- Calculated in real-time and updated every 15 seconds

Composition:
The index is heavily weighted toward multinational companies in sectors like oil & gas, mining, banking, pharmaceuticals, and consumer goods. Many constituents earn significant revenue outside the UK.

Major Companies:
Leading constituents include Shell, AstraZeneca, HSBC, Unilever, BP, Diageo, GSK, Rio Tinto, British American Tobacco, and Glencore.

International Exposure:
Despite being a UK index, approximately 70% of FTSE 100 revenues come from overseas operations, making it sensitive to global economic conditions and currency fluctuations.

Performance:
The FTSE 100 has historically provided lower returns than U.S. indices, partly due to sector composition with fewer high-growth tech companies and more traditional industries.

"Footsie":
The index is colloquially known as "Footsie" in financial media. It's denominated in British pounds but international investors must consider exchange rate impacts.

Investment:
Investors can gain exposure through ETFs like VUKE (Vanguard FTSE 100), ISF (iShares Core FTSE 100), and EWU (iShares MSCI United Kingdom ETF).
