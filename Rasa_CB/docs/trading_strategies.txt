Trading Strategies Guide

Overview of Trading Styles:
Different trading strategies suit different personalities, time availability, and risk tolerances. Understanding various approaches helps you find what works best for you.

Day Trading:

What is Day Trading?
- Opening and closing positions within the same trading day
- No overnight positions held
- Requires significant time commitment
- Focus on short-term price movements

Characteristics:
- Time Frame: Minutes to hours
- Trades per Day: 10-100+
- Capital Required: Higher (US PDT rule: $25,000 minimum)
- Time Commitment: Full-time
- Profit Targets: Small gains per trade

Day Trading Strategies:
- Scalping: Very short trades for tiny profits
- Momentum: Riding strong price moves
- Breakout: Trading range breakouts
- Reversal: Catching trend reversals
- News Trading: Trading on news releases

Pros:
- No overnight risk
- Fast feedback on strategies
- Potential for daily income

Cons:
- High stress and screen time
- Transaction costs add up
- Requires discipline and experience
- Most day traders lose money

Swing Trading:

What is Swing Trading?
- Holding positions for days to weeks
- Capturing "swings" in price
- Balance between active and passive

Characteristics:
- Time Frame: Days to weeks
- Trades per Month: 5-20
- Capital Required: Moderate
- Time Commitment: Part-time (1-2 hours/day)
- Profit Targets: 5-20% per trade

Swing Trading Strategies:
- Trend Following: Trade in direction of trend
- Counter-Trend: Trade pullbacks in trends
- Breakout/Breakdown: Trade range breaks
- Mean Reversion: Fade extreme moves

Pros:
- Less time-intensive than day trading
- Larger profit potential per trade
- Works with full-time job

Cons:
- Overnight/weekend risk
- Requires patience
- May miss intraday opportunities

Position Trading / Investing:

What is Position Trading?
- Holding for weeks to months
- Focus on major trends and fundamentals
- Closer to traditional investing

Characteristics:
- Time Frame: Weeks to months
- Trades per Year: 10-50
- Capital Required: Variable
- Time Commitment: Low (few hours/week)
- Profit Targets: 20%+ per position

Strategies:
- Buy and Hold: Long-term value investing
- Trend Following: Ride major trends
- Growth Investing: Focus on high-growth companies
- Value Investing: Buy undervalued assets
- Dividend Investing: Focus on income

Pros:
- Minimal time required
- Lower transaction costs
- Compound growth potential

Cons:
- Capital tied up longer
- Requires patience
- May miss short-term opportunities

HODL Strategy (Crypto):

What is HODLing?
- Long-term holding regardless of volatility
- "Hold On for Dear Life"
- Common in cryptocurrency markets

Approach:
- Buy and hold for years
- Ignore short-term volatility
- Dollar-cost average into positions
- Cold storage for security

Best For:
- Believers in long-term crypto adoption
- Those without time for active trading
- Tax-advantaged holding (long-term capital gains)

Common Trading Strategies:

Trend Following:
- Identify and trade with the trend
- "The trend is your friend"
- Use moving averages for direction
- Enter on pullbacks, exit when trend breaks

Breakout Trading:
- Enter when price breaks key levels
- Volume confirmation important
- Set stops below breakout point
- Target based on pattern measurement

Mean Reversion:
- Trade extreme deviations from average
- Use RSI, Bollinger Bands for signals
- Works in ranging markets
- Fails in strong trends

Momentum Trading:
- Buy strength, sell weakness
- "High flying stocks tend to fly higher"
- Use relative strength for selection
- Quick to exit when momentum fades

Arbitrage:
- Exploit price differences across markets
- Requires speed and low costs
- Examples: exchange arbitrage, funding rate arbitrage
- Mostly done by algorithms now

Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA):
- Invest fixed amount at regular intervals
- Reduces timing risk
- Works well for long-term investing
- Automates the investment process

Grid Trading:
- Place buy orders below current price
- Place sell orders above current price
- Profits from ranging markets
- Can be automated with bots

Algorithmic/Systematic Trading:
- Rules-based approach
- Backtested on historical data
- Removes emotion from trading
- Requires programming skills or platforms

Building Your Strategy:

1. Define Your Edge:
- What gives you an advantage?
- Backtested performance
- Logical market reasoning

2. Set Clear Rules:
- Entry criteria
- Exit criteria (profit and loss)
- Position sizing rules
- Maximum positions

3. Backtest and Paper Trade:
- Test on historical data
- Paper trade in real-time
- Adjust and refine

4. Start Small:
- Begin with small positions
- Scale up as confidence grows
- Track all trades

5. Review and Improve:
- Regular performance review
- Identify what works/doesn't
- Continuous improvement

Key Success Factors:
1. Discipline to follow rules
2. Proper risk management
3. Realistic expectations
4. Continuous learning
5. Emotional control
6. Adequate capital
7. Right strategy for your personality
