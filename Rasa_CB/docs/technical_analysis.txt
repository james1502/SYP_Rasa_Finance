Technical Analysis Guide

What is Technical Analysis?
Technical analysis is a method of evaluating securities by analyzing statistics generated by market activity, such as past prices and volume. Technical analysts believe that historical trading activity and price changes can be valuable indicators of future price movements.

Core Principles:
1. Price discounts everything (all information reflected in price)
2. Prices move in trends
3. History tends to repeat itself
4. Market action is driven by crowd psychology

Chart Types:

Line Charts:
- Connect closing prices
- Simple and clean view
- Good for identifying trends

Bar Charts (OHLC):
- Open, High, Low, Close for each period
- Vertical bar shows range
- Horizontal ticks show open/close

Candlestick Charts:
- Visual representation of price action
- Body shows open-close range
- Wicks show high-low range
- Green/white: Close > Open (bullish)
- Red/black: Close < Open (bearish)

Trend Analysis:

Types of Trends:
- Uptrend: Higher highs and higher lows
- Downtrend: Lower highs and lower lows
- Sideways/Range: No clear direction

Trendlines:
- Connect swing lows in uptrend
- Connect swing highs in downtrend
- Break of trendline signals potential reversal

Support and Resistance:
- Support: Price level where buying emerges
- Resistance: Price level where selling emerges
- Previous support becomes resistance (and vice versa)

Key Technical Indicators:

Moving Averages:
- Simple Moving Average (SMA): Average of closing prices
- Exponential Moving Average (EMA): Weights recent prices more
- Common periods: 20, 50, 100, 200 days
- Golden Cross: Short MA crosses above long MA (bullish)
- Death Cross: Short MA crosses below long MA (bearish)

Relative Strength Index (RSI):
- Momentum oscillator (0-100 scale)
- Overbought: Above 70
- Oversold: Below 30
- Divergence signals potential reversal
- Default period: 14

Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD):
- Trend-following momentum indicator
- MACD Line: 12 EMA - 26 EMA
- Signal Line: 9 EMA of MACD line
- Histogram: Difference between MACD and Signal
- Crossovers indicate buy/sell signals

Bollinger Bands:
- Middle band: 20-period SMA
- Upper band: SMA + 2 standard deviations
- Lower band: SMA - 2 standard deviations
- Price touching bands indicates potential reversal
- Band squeeze suggests volatility expansion coming

Volume Indicators:
- Volume: Number of shares/contracts traded
- On-Balance Volume (OBV): Cumulative volume flow
- Volume Profile: Shows volume at price levels
- High volume confirms price moves

Fibonacci Retracements:
- Key levels: 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6%
- Used to identify potential support/resistance
- Based on Fibonacci sequence ratios
- Applied to significant price swings

Chart Patterns:

Reversal Patterns:
- Head and Shoulders: Bearish reversal
- Inverse Head and Shoulders: Bullish reversal
- Double Top/Bottom: Reversal patterns
- Triple Top/Bottom: Stronger reversal signals

Continuation Patterns:
- Flags and Pennants: Brief consolidation
- Triangles: Ascending, Descending, Symmetrical
- Rectangles: Horizontal consolidation
- Wedges: Rising or Falling

Candlestick Patterns:
- Doji: Indecision (open = close)
- Hammer/Hanging Man: Potential reversal
- Engulfing: Strong reversal signal
- Morning/Evening Star: Three-candle reversal

Time Frames:
- Intraday: 1-min, 5-min, 15-min, 1-hour
- Daily: Most common for swing trading
- Weekly: Longer-term trends
- Monthly: Major trends and cycles

Multiple Time Frame Analysis:
- Analyze higher time frame for trend
- Use lower time frame for entry
- Alignment increases probability

Limitations of Technical Analysis:
1. Self-fulfilling prophecy debates
2. Doesn't account for fundamentals
3. Past performance != future results
4. Requires experience and skill
5. Signals can be subjective
6. Whipsaws in ranging markets

Best Practices:
1. Use multiple indicators for confirmation
2. Combine with fundamental analysis
3. Always use stop losses
4. Practice on paper before real trading
5. Keep a trading journal
6. Don't overcomplicate - simpler often better
