CAC 40 Index (France) Guide

What is the CAC 40?
The CAC 40 (Cotation Assistée en Continu) is France's benchmark stock market index, representing the 40 largest and most actively traded companies on Euronext Paris. It's one of Europe's most important market indicators.

Key Facts:
- Founded: December 31, 1987
- Base Value: 1,000 points
- Operator: Euronext
- Constituents: 40 companies
- Calculation: Free-float market capitalization weighted
- Review: Quarterly rebalancing

Index Composition by Sector:
- Luxury Goods & Consumer
- Industrials
- Healthcare & Pharmaceuticals
- Energy
- Financial Services
- Technology
- Telecommunications
- Utilities

Major CAC 40 Constituents:
- LVMH (Luxury goods - largest weight)
- L'Oréal (Cosmetics)
- Hermès (Luxury)
- TotalEnergies (Energy)
- Sanofi (Pharmaceuticals)
- Airbus (Aerospace)
- Schneider Electric (Electrical)
- Air Liquide (Industrial gases)
- BNP Paribas (Banking)
- Kering (Luxury - Gucci, YSL)
- Dassault Systèmes (Software)
- Safran (Aerospace)

Trading Hours:
- Continuous Trading: 9:00 AM - 5:30 PM (CET)
- Opening Auction: 7:15 AM - 9:00 AM (CET)
- Closing Auction: 5:30 PM - 5:35 PM (CET)

CAC Index Family:
- CAC 40: Main large-cap index
- CAC Next 20: Next 20 largest companies
- CAC Mid 60: Mid-cap index
- CAC Small: Small-cap companies
- SBF 120: Broader 120-company index
- CAC 40 ESG: ESG-screened version

Unique Characteristics:
Luxury Sector Dominance:
- LVMH, Hermès, Kering, L'Oréal represent significant weight
- French luxury brands are global leaders
- Makes CAC 40 sensitive to global luxury demand
- China consumer spending particularly important

Factors Affecting the CAC 40:
1. Global luxury goods demand
2. ECB monetary policy
3. Euro exchange rates
4. Chinese consumer spending
5. Energy prices (TotalEnergies impact)
6. French economic data
7. Tourism trends
8. Global aerospace demand

Economic Significance:
- France is Eurozone's 2nd largest economy
- Strong in luxury, aerospace, and nuclear energy
- Major tourism destination
- Significant agricultural sector

Advantages:
- Exposure to global luxury brands
- Diversified sector mix
- High-quality multinational companies
- Strong dividend culture
- Euro-denominated

Risks:
- Luxury demand sensitivity (cyclical)
- Euro currency fluctuations
- French labor regulations
- Political uncertainty
- Energy transition challenges

Historical Performance:
- 1987: Index launched
- 2000: Dot-com peak around 6,800
- 2008-2009: Financial crisis lows
- 2021+: New all-time highs
- Generally tracks European sentiment

Dividend Yield:
- CAC 40 typically yields 2-3%
- French companies known for dividends
- Many constituents have long dividend histories
- Tax considerations for international investors

Comparison with Other Indices:
- DAX (Germany): More industrial/auto
- FTSE 100 (UK): More commodities/finance
- CAC 40: Unique luxury exposure
- IBEX 35 (Spain): Smaller, banking-heavy

Corporate Governance:
- Strong shareholder protections
- EU regulatory framework
- Increasing ESG focus
- Board diversity requirements
